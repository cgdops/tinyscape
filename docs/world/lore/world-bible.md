# TinyScape World Bible

**Status:** direction `approved` (Shipwright's Coast skeleton + Tithe weather). The specific names,
dates and details below are my calls filling in that direction — treat them as `draft` until you
object to any of them. Supersedes nothing; the three-way pitch that led here is kept at
[world-bible-proposals.md](world-bible-proposals.md).

---

## 1. The premise

The **Tallow Coast** is a working frontier: a ragged coastline, a scatter of islands, and an old
forest interior nobody has finished mapping. People came here within living memory for timber, ore
and fish. There is no king. There are charters, guilds, harbourmasters, and a great deal of arguing
about docking fees.

That is the world people *run*. Underneath it is the world they *live in*: the land here yields
more than it should, and it keeps accounts. Trees come back thick. Ore seams run richer than the
assay says they ought to. And every settlement that has lasted more than a generation observes the
same small courtesies — something left at the stump, a phrase said at the well, days when nobody
cuts. Nobody calls it magic. They call it **the Custom**, and they'd rather you just did it.

The tension between those two worlds is the engine of the setting. The guilds are efficient,
recent, and think the Custom is charming superstition. The villages are old, correct, and have the
healthy woodland to show for it. Both are right about something.

**The player** is an ordinary newcomer with a rucksack and no reputation: not a chosen one, not
heir to anything. You get on by learning trades and by not being an idiot about the Custom.

## 2. Geography

Name only what's needed; the map grows with the build.

- **The Tallow Coast** — the settled mainland shore. Named for the oil trade that first paid for
  ships out here. Wet, green, working.
- **The Scatter** — the islands offshore. Fishing stations, quarries, a few charter towns, and
  further out, places the charts stop being confident.
- **The Longwood** — the forest interior behind the coast. Genuinely old. The Custom is strongest
  here and the guilds' authority is thinnest. This is where the setting keeps its mysteries.
- **The Sallow** — the river running out of the Longwood to the sea. Timber floats down it. It is
  the reason Willowmere exists.
- **Harrowick** — the port at the Sallow's mouth, a day downriver. Where timber becomes money:
  harbourmaster, Assayers' hall, Harbour Court, and the Revenue Office everyone loathes.
- **Ardmoor** — the mainland proper, far off, old and administrative. Sends inspectors and tax
  collectors. Nobody out here has been there.

Rule of thumb: **the coast is where you earn, the Longwood is where the world gets strange, and
Ardmoor is a punchline.**

## 3. Timeline

Deliberately shallow — no ancient prophecy, and everything is within reach of someone's grandmother.

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| ~80 years ago | First permanent settlement on the Tallow Coast. Hard, unglamorous, half of it failed. |
| ~55 years ago | Ardmoor issues the first **charters**, inventing the guilds by accident. |
| ~30 years ago | The **Harbour Courts** established to stop the charters killing each other. |
| ~12 years ago | **The Stillyear** — one season the Longwood gave nothing. No explanation was ever agreed. The old villages say the accounts came due; the charters say it was blight. Both stopped talking about it. |
| Now | Timber demand rising. New charters pushing further up the Sallow than anyone has cut before. |

The Stillyear is the setting's loaded gun. Do not explain it in early content.

## 4. Factions

**The Timber Charter.** Ranks, quotas, contracts, and a genuine pride in the trade. Your most
likely first employer. Officially neutral on the Custom; individual foremen range from devout to
openly contemptuous.

**The Assayers' Guild.** Certify ore, timber grade and weights. Relentlessly, gloriously petty.
Trusted precisely because they're insufferable. Comic relief that occasionally turns out to be
right about something serious.

**The Deepwater Fishers.** Oldest working charter, most superstitious of the guilds, because the
sea has always kept accounts and they never needed convincing.

**The Harbour Courts.** Arbitration, licences, fines. Slow, procedural, and the closest thing to
law. Quest structure lives here: disputes need evidence, and evidence needs someone to go and get it.

**The Revenue Office of Ardmoor.** Universally loathed. Not villains — just correct at the wrong
moments.

**The Wardens of the Verge.** Not a guild. Boundary-walkers who keep the hedges, the stones and the
forms along the edge of the Longwood. Unpaid, unofficial, taken very seriously by anyone who has
lived here long. Your route into the Custom.

**The Free Cut.** Not an organisation — an attitude, mostly among young charter hands. Cut without
offering, take the extra yield, and so far get away with it. Sympathetic: they aren't wrong that
the Custom costs time, and they're going to be a problem.

## 5. The Custom (how the strange works)

Rules, so it stays consistent:

1. **It is not spellcasting.** No incantations, no chosen bloodlines. It is etiquette that happens
   to work.
2. **It is transactional and local.** A grove, a seam, a stretch of water. You keep accounts with
   *that place*, not with a god. There is no pantheon; leave the question of what's owed unanswered.
3. **It is generous by default.** The land gives well. Neglect degrades slowly, over seasons, not
   instantly — so it is never a per-swing tax on the player.
4. **Nobody has the full picture.** The Wardens know the forms. The Assayers can measure the
   effects and can't explain them. Anyone claiming a complete theory is selling something.
5. **The Longwood is where it is loudest.** Distance from the coast is the difficulty dial.

**Player magic:** yes, eventually, and it is a *licensed trade* rather than a fantasy arsenal —
practical work like preserving cargo, reading currents, keeping a lamp lit, charting. Nothing
early-game, nothing flashy. This is my default call; say the word if you'd rather magic stay
entirely out of the player's hands.

## 6. Skills and the economy

Skills are **trades**, and the guilds are the reason they matter.

- A skill level is also a **guild rank** — not just a number, but who will now do business with you,
  which contracts you can take, and which docks you can walk onto.
- Every gathering skill feeds a production skill feeds a consumer. Timber → shipwrights. Ore →
  assay → smithing. Fish → provisioning for crews going out to the Scatter.
- The Custom sits *on top* of skills as mastery, not as cost. Knowing the forms means better
  standing with a place: healthier groves, richer seams, and access to ground that won't tolerate
  the careless. A high-level woodcutter isn't just faster — they're *welcome*.

**Woodcutting is the founding trade.** It's the reason the coast was settled, the reason Willowmere
exists, and the first skill in the build. The player's first swing should carry both halves of the
setting: a contract to fill, and a small courtesy at the stump.

## 7. Willowmere

An inland timber village a day up the Sallow from Harrowick. Old for this coast — about sixty
years — and quietly proud of it. It has kept the forms since it was founded and has the woodland to
show for it, which the villagers will mention.

Mapping onto what's actually built:

- **The pond** — a holding pond for felled logs waiting on the float downriver. Working
  infrastructure, not scenery.
- **The footbridge** — where the log road crosses to the cutting side.
- **The well** — the oldest thing in the village and half a shrine. This is where Willowmere's
  Custom is observed. It is also just the well.
- **The market stall** — trades in the ordinary and the ceremonial without distinguishing between
  them: rope, salt, nails, and the small goods people leave at stumps.
- **The cottage** — someone specific lives here. Cast to be designed.

Willowmere's dramatic tension in one line: **a village that does things properly, on a river the
Timber Charter now wants to cut much harder.** That's enough to hang a starting quest line on
without inventing anything else.

## 8. Tone and naming

**Voice.** Warm, dry, concrete. Small problems that matter to the people who have them. Humour
comes from character and from bureaucracy, never from winking at the player. The uncanny is
underplayed — people are polite about it, which is what makes it land.

**Never:** chosen-one framing, ancient evil stirring, grimdark, modern slang, lore that exists only
to justify a mechanic, or an NPC explaining the Custom in full.

**Names.** Plain and English-textured, ideally meaning something workmanlike: *Willowmere,
Harrowick, the Sallow, the Scatter, Bracken Quay, Stint*. Compound place names of
landscape + function. People get short working names — Hale, Mabb, Oster, Truet, Winnow. Avoid
apostrophes, invented phonemes and fantasy vowels entirely.

**Institutions** get dry, slightly self-important names: the Timber Charter, the Assayers' Guild,
the Harbour Court, the Revenue Office. **Folk things** get plain ones: the Custom, the Verge,
the Stillyear, the Free Cut.

## 9. Deliberately unresolved

Do not answer these in early content; they're the setting's long fuse.

- What the Stillyear actually was.
- What, if anything, is on the receiving end of the Custom.
- How far the Longwood goes, and what's charted at the edge of the Scatter.
- Whether the Free Cut are going to be proven right.

## 10. Open decisions

1. Does the player learn magic at all (my default: yes, late, and as a mundane licensed trade)?
2. Willowmere's cast — how many named NPCs for the first slice? I'd suggest four.
3. Is the first quest line a **Timber Charter contract** (trade-facing) or a **Warden errand**
   (Custom-facing)? This sets which half of the setting the player meets first.
